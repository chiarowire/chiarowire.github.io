Gemini computer use is a new feature in Google's Gemini app for Mac, announced on November 19, 2026. You describe a task, and Gemini clicks, types and moves through your Mac apps, files and websites to finish it, in a separate background window. It's US-only, English-only, and needs a paid Google AI plan.

## What happened with Gemini on Mac

Google announced the feature in a [post on its official blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/computer-use-mac/). "Computer use" is the industry's name for an AI that operates a computer the way a person does: opening apps, pressing buttons, filling in boxes and picking menu items, instead of only writing a reply in a chat window.

Google's examples are the kind of chores people put off. Pulling weights and addresses out of PDF files to fill in a shipping quote. Gathering numbers from online dashboards and building a slide deck from them. Looking through a folder of clothing photos, checking prices and drafting resale listings. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/19/google-gives-gemini-the-ability-to-use-your-mac-for-you/) adds simpler ones, like sorting files into folders or repeating the same step across a batch of files.

Here's who can use it at launch, based on Google's announcement and TechCrunch:

| Question | Answer at launch |
|---|---|
| Where? | United States only |
| Language? | English only |
| Which plans? | Google AI Plus, Pro or Ultra |
| Which accounts? | Personal Google accounts, users 18 or older |
| Which Macs? | Apple Silicon Macs on macOS Sequoia 15.0 or later |
| Where do I get it? | The Gemini app for Mac, from Google |

Google didn't say when it will reach other countries, languages or computers, TechCrunch reports.

## How it stays in check

Handing an AI your mouse and keyboard sounds risky, so Google built in limits. According to its blog post:

- Gemini works in an **isolated background window**, so your own screen and cursor stay free while it runs.
- A **live preview** in the chat shows each step as it happens.
- **You choose which apps** Gemini is allowed to use.
- You can **pause or stop** it at any time.
- It asks you to **approve key actions** before it takes them.

In other words, it's built to be watched, not left alone.

## What it means for you

If you don't pay for a Google AI plan, or you don't use a Mac, nothing changes for you yet.

If you do, Gemini on Mac can now do more than answer questions. It can carry out a multi-step job across several apps while you keep working on something else. The catch is that you still need to check what it did, and approve the important steps. An AI that misreads a form can put the wrong thing in the wrong box. (Our explainer on [how ChatGPT works](../how-does-chatgpt-work/) covers why AI tools sometimes get things confidently wrong.)

Google isn't first here. TechCrunch notes that OpenAI, Anthropic and Meta already offer agent features in their desktop apps, and calls Google "a little late to the game." An "agent" is simply an AI that takes actions for you instead of only chatting.

## Is it worth caring about?

Our take: yes, as a sign of where everyday AI is going, even if you never turn it on.

Recent launches show a pattern. Google just released a new model, [Gemini 4 Neon](../gemini-4-neon/), for its paid plans. OpenAI recently let its agents [check out and pay for things](../chatgpt-wallet/) with your approval. And Gemini is spreading into [cars](../gemini-carplay/) and [glasses](../google-gemini-smart-glasses/). Assistants are moving from talking to doing, and the computer you already own is the next place that's happening.

Who this matters to most right now: Mac users in the US who already pay for a Google AI plan and spend time on repetitive computer chores. For everyone else, it's an early look at a kind of feature several big AI companies are now building. Whether it saves real time will depend on how often it gets tasks right without a human fixing them, and that's something only use over time will show.
