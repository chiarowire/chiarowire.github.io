WhatsApp scheduled messages are here. Announced on November 25, 2026, the feature lets you write a message now and have it sent anywhere from 10 minutes to 14 days later, in one-on-one chats and group chats. It works for text, photos and videos, and WhatsApp says it's rolling out to everyone worldwide over the coming weeks.

## What happened with WhatsApp scheduled messages

WhatsApp calls scheduling "one of the most-requested features" from its users, according to [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2328242/whatsapp-is-finally-offering-scheduled-messages/). It was spotted in beta versions of the app earlier this year, and now it's official, as reported by [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/25/whatsapp-finally-adds-scheduled-messages/) and [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/whatsapp-now-lets-you-schedule-messages-in-group-and-individual-chats/).

Here's how it works, based on those reports:

| What | Details |
|---|---|
| How to schedule | Type your message, then press and hold the send button and pick a date and time |
| How far ahead | From 10 minutes up to 14 days |
| What you can send | Text, photos and videos |
| Where | One-on-one chats and group chats |
| Changing your mind | You can see all your upcoming scheduled messages and delete them before they go out |
| Phone off? | It still sends, even if your phone is off or has no signal |
| What the other person sees | A normal message, with no sign it was scheduled |
| Who gets it | iPhone and Android users worldwide, rolling out over the coming weeks |

TechCrunch notes that it isn't clear yet whether you can change the send time of a message once it's scheduled. The reports describe deleting one, so for now the safe assumption is that you'd delete it and schedule it again.

## What it means for you

- **No more late-night reminders to yourself.** If you want a birthday message to land right at midnight, or a "good luck" text to arrive the morning of someone's interview, you can write it whenever it's convenient and let the app send it. Those are examples WhatsApp itself gives, per Engadget.
- **Time zones get easier.** Messaging family or friends abroad? You can write during your day and have it arrive during theirs, instead of buzzing their phone at 3 a.m.
- **It stays private.** WhatsApp says scheduled messages are end-to-end encrypted like any other personal chat. That means only you and the people in the chat can read them, not WhatsApp. MacRumors and Engadget both report this.
- **Your phone doesn't need to be on.** Because the message sends even if your device is offline, you can schedule something before a flight and it will still go out on time.
- **You may not see it yet.** "Rolling out over the coming weeks" means some people get it before others. If the press-and-hold option isn't there yet, it should show up as the rollout reaches you.

## Is it worth caring about?

*Our take:* yes, if you use WhatsApp every day, because it's a small change to something huge numbers of people already do.

- **It matters most to busy group chats and far-flung families.** Anyone who coordinates plans, sends reminders or keeps in touch across time zones gets a simple tool that saves a bit of mental effort.
- **It's a catch-up, not a first.** Apple's iMessage already has a Send Later option, MacRumors points out, and TechCrunch notes that Telegram and Signal have offered scheduling for a while. Instagram, which like WhatsApp is owned by Meta, has had it since 2024, per Engadget. So this mostly brings WhatsApp in line with other apps.
- **It's part of a busy month for Meta's apps.** Meta also just put its Muse AI agent [inside Instagram DMs](../meta-muse-instagram-dms/). Scheduled messages are much simpler than that: no AI involved, just your words sent at the time you picked.
