Google released Gemini 4 Neon on November 18, 2026. It's a new AI model that sits in the middle of the Gemini 4 family: smarter than the fast everyday model, cheaper to run than the top one. If you pay for Google AI Pro or Ultra, you can pick Gemini 4 Neon in the Gemini app now. Free users aren't mentioned.

## What happened with Gemini 4 Neon

Google announced the model in a [post on its official blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-neon/). The Gemini 4 family now has three main members, and Google describes each one's job like this:

| Model | Google's description of its job |
|---|---|
| Gemini 4 Carbon | Your "hardest, long-horizon projects" |
| Gemini 4 Neon (new) | Balances depth and efficiency |
| Gemini 4 Hydrogen | "Fast everyday execution" |

In plain words: Carbon is the heavyweight, Hydrogen is the quick one, and Neon is meant to get close to Carbon's smarts while costing less to run. Google says that, at its default setting, Neon costs 32% less per task than Carbon. That figure is about what it costs to run the model, mostly relevant to companies paying per use. It isn't a change to what you pay for a subscription.

Google says Neon is rolling out in several places at once: the Gemini app, its tools for developers, and Gemini for business customers.

## What it means for you

If you use the Gemini app on your phone or computer, here's what Google says changes:

- **Who gets it:** Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers. Google's post says you can select Gemini 4 Neon "from the model drop-down in the Gemini app." The post doesn't say anything about the free version.
- **What it's for:** Google says Neon is good at work that needs careful reasoning but quick answers. In the app, it points to better writing and summaries from files and photos you upload, research that draws on current web sources, and pulling details from Google apps like Gmail and Docs.
- **Longer jobs:** Google says Tasks in Gemini, its feature for multi-step jobs, can now run on Neon. Its examples include going through billing statements and cross-checking receipts.

If you've never switched models before: many chatbots now let you choose which "brain" answers you, usually from a menu at the top of the chat. A bigger model tends to think longer and handle harder questions; a smaller one answers faster. (Our explainer on [how ChatGPT-style chatbots work](../how-does-chatgpt-work/) covers what a "model" actually is.)

## Fewer made-up answers?

One part of Google's announcement is worth a closer look, because it's a common complaint about AI tools: chatbots sometimes invent things. Google says Neon is trained to finish multi-step work "without cutting corners, fabricating results, or overstepping user boundaries."

Google backs that up with its own test scores, including a 99.2% pass rate on what it calls its agent honesty evaluation. Keep in mind these are Google's own tests of its own model. They haven't been checked by anyone else in what we've read, so treat them as Google's claim rather than a guarantee. Any AI model can still get things wrong, so it's still worth double-checking anything important.

## Is it worth caring about?

*Our take:* it depends on how you use Gemini.

- **If you pay for Google AI Pro or Ultra,** this is a new option in your model menu, aimed at bigger jobs like summarizing long documents or working through a pile of receipts. It's worth knowing it's there.
- **If you use free Gemini,** nothing in Google's announcement changes for you today.
- **If you don't use Gemini at all,** the bigger picture is that AI companies keep adding middle-tier models that aim for "almost the best, for less." That's how better AI tends to reach more people over time. Gemini itself keeps spreading too, from [your car](../gemini-carplay/) to [smart glasses](../google-gemini-smart-glasses/) to [using apps on your Mac](../gemini-mac-computer-use/).

One more thing, if you're about to hand Gemini your bills or email to sort through: Google recently changed [how Gemini saves your chat history and uses it for AI training](../gemini-chat-history-ai-training/). It's worth a look at those settings first.
