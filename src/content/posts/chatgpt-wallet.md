ChatGPT Wallet is a new OpenAI feature, launched on November 13, 2026, that lets ChatGPT's AI agents, called dots, pay for things on your behalf. You save a card once, the agent finds what you asked for, and you approve every purchase. For now it's limited to some ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the US.

## What happened with ChatGPT Wallet

OpenAI announced ChatGPT Wallet on Friday, November 13, according to [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/13/chatgpts-new-wallet-lets-ai-agents-shop-and-check-out-for-you/) and [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/13/openai-chatgpt-wallet-dot-purchases.html). It works with dots, the personal AI agents OpenAI introduced at its developer conference in late September (CNBC). An "agent" here just means an AI that can carry out tasks for you, like searching shops and filling in a checkout page, instead of only answering questions.

Here's the launch in one table, based on TechCrunch, CNBC and [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2316137/you-can-now-tempt-fate-and-give-your-banking-information-to-chatgpt/):

| Question | Answer at launch |
|---|---|
| Who can use it? | US ChatGPT Pro subscribers, 18 or older, rolling out gradually |
| Which cards? | US-issued Visa, Mastercard and American Express |
| Where does it work? | Only inside a conversation with a dot |
| Who approves purchases? | You, every time, with a check like Face ID or a fingerprint |
| Does OpenAI take a cut? | Not for now, CNBC and Engadget report |

## How it works

The flow is simple. You add a card once. You ask your dot to find something, and it gathers the items. Before checkout, you see the items, the total and which card will be charged, and you approve it, TechCrunch reports.

The agent never sees your real card number. Instead, the card networks hand it a one-time stand-in, called a payment token, with its own number, security code and expiry date that only works for that one purchase (CNBC). The store gets that token, not your actual card. The dot also passes along what the store needs to ship the order, like your address.

OpenAI says you keep your card's usual rewards and protections, including the ability to dispute a charge, according to TechCrunch and CNBC. Engadget notes that each approval creates a record of the purchase.

## What it means for you

If you don't pay for ChatGPT Pro, nothing changes yet. This launch is deliberately small. American Express told CNBC that OpenAI can widen it later, since ChatGPT has more than 1.2 billion weekly users.

If you do get access, the main thing to know is that the AI can do the legwork, but it can't spend your money without you. Every purchase still waits for your approval.

It also won't work everywhere. Some stores block AI agents from their sites. CNBC reports that Amazon doesn't let AI agents buy on its platform and that United Airlines said it will block agents from booking tickets. OpenAI has published guidance to help stores that want to accept agent shopping.

## Is it worth caring about?

Our take: yes, as a sign of where AI assistants are heading, even if you never use this version.

For a few years, chatbots have mostly talked. (Our explainer on [how ChatGPT works](../how-does-chatgpt-work/) covers the "predict the next word" trick behind that.) The next step the big AI companies are working toward is assistants that *do* things: book, order, pay. Paying is the hardest part to get right, because mistakes cost real money. That's why the launch keeps a person in the loop for every purchase. (OpenAI's newest ChatGPT models, [GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol](../gpt-6-2-astra-sol/), followed a few days later.)

OpenAI isn't alone in pushing assistants into everyday tasks. Apple has been widening access to its [new Siri AI](../siri-ai-waitlist/), and Google has been putting Gemini into [cars](../gemini-carplay/) and [glasses](../google-gemini-smart-glasses/). ChatGPT Wallet is one of the first big launches that lets an assistant actually check out. (Google has since let Gemini [work in your Mac apps for you](../gemini-mac-computer-use/), too, and connected its AI Mode search to [Google Wallet in read-only form](../google-ai-mode-wallet-drive/), and Meta has put its [Muse agent inside Instagram DMs](../meta-muse-instagram-dms/).)

Who this matters to most right now: ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the US, and online stores deciding whether to let AI agents through their doors. For everyone else, it's an early look at a feature that, if it works, could show up in more assistants later. OpenAI itself calls agent shopping "early days," per CNBC.

## What's next

OpenAI has said it plans to add spending limits, settings for purchase categories or time periods, and pre-approved purchases that agents can complete on their own, plus support at more retailers, TechCrunch reports. No dates have been announced for any of it.
