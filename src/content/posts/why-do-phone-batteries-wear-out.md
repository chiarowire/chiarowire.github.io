Why do phone batteries wear out? Every lithium-ion battery slowly loses capacity as it's used, a little with each full charge cycle, and heat speeds that up. So a two-year-old phone holds less energy than it did new. Apple designs recent iPhone batteries to keep 80% of their original capacity after 1,000 full cycles.

That's the short answer. Here's what's going on, what the Battery Health screen is telling you, and when any of it matters.

## Why phone batteries wear out at all

Almost every phone, laptop and smartwatch runs on a lithium-ion battery. Apple's [battery explainer](https://www.apple.com/batteries/why-lithium-ion/) says the type is popular because it charges faster, lasts longer and packs more power into less weight than older battery types.

The trade-off is that no rechargeable battery lasts forever. In Apple's words, "the capacity of any type of battery will diminish after a certain amount of recharging." For lithium-ion, the loss is small each time, which is why you don't notice it day to day. You notice it a year or two in, when the phone that used to last until bedtime needs a top-up by late afternoon.

## What a charge cycle actually is

A "charge cycle" sounds like plugging in once and unplugging once. It isn't. According to [Apple](https://www.apple.com/batteries/why-lithium-ion/), one cycle means using up 100% of the battery's capacity in total, not all at once.

If you use 75% of your battery today, charge it overnight, then use 25% tomorrow, that adds up to one cycle. Topping up from 60% to 90% doesn't count as a whole cycle on its own. That's also why you don't need to run your phone down to zero before charging it: Apple's page says plainly that it isn't necessary.

Here's how Apple describes its design targets for iPhone, from its [iPhone battery and performance](https://support.apple.com/en-us/101575) support page:

| iPhone models | Designed to keep 80% of original capacity after |
|---|---|
| iPhone 14 and earlier | 500 full charge cycles |
| iPhone 15 and later | 1,000 full charge cycles |

Apple says these figures assume ideal conditions. Real-world results vary with how and where the phone is used.

## Heat is the battery's biggest enemy

Use is one thing that wears a battery down. Temperature is the other. Apple's [battery performance page](https://www.apple.com/batteries/maximizing-performance/) says its phones, tablets and watches are most comfortable between 62° and 72°F (16° to 22°C), and that heat above 95°F (35°C) can permanently reduce battery capacity. Charging while it's hot can do extra damage.

Cold works differently. It can make your battery drain faster in the moment, but Apple says the effect is temporary and the battery goes back to normal once it warms up.

This is also why your phone sometimes pauses charging at 80%. Apple's batteries charge quickly to about 80% and then slow down, and software may hold off on charging past 80% when the battery is too warm. A feature called Optimized Battery Charging also learns your routine and may wait to finish charging until shortly before you usually unplug.

## What the Battery Health number means

On an iPhone, Settings shows a "Maximum Capacity" percentage. Apple says it measures your battery's capacity compared with when it was new. At 100%, it holds what it did on day one. At 85%, a full charge holds about 85% as much, so you get fewer hours between charges.

There's a second effect that matters more for some people. As a battery ages, it also gets worse at delivering quick bursts of power. Apple's support page explains that an aged battery may not keep up with a sudden demand, so the phone can shut itself off to protect its parts, even when the screen says there's charge left. To prevent repeat shutdowns, iOS may then limit peak performance, which can mean slower app launches or lower speaker volume. Apple says this kicks in only after an unexpected shutdown has happened on a battery in that state.

## What it means for you

- **Shorter battery life on an older phone is normal.** It's chemistry, not a sign something broke.
- **Slowdowns and surprise shutdowns can be battery-related.** On iPhone, the Battery Health screen says when performance management has turned on.
- **Where you leave your phone matters.** A hot car or a sunny dashboard does more harm than how often you plug in.

## Is it worth caring about?

Our take: it matters most to people who keep a phone for three years or more, and to anyone whose phone shuts down at 20%. If you upgrade every year or two, you may never notice. Either way, knowing what the number means makes it easier to understand what your phone is telling you.
