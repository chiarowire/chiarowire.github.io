Apple HomeView arrives on Friday, November 13. It's a $299 smart display: a 7-inch touchscreen on a speaker base, or a version that mounts on a wall. It runs Siri AI, plays music and video, makes FaceTime calls and controls smart home gear. It isn't sold in the European Union. Pre-orders have been open since October 13.

Here's what Apple HomeView does, what you need to use it, and who it's likely to matter to.

## What happened

Apple announced HomeView on October 13 in a [press release](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/10/meet-apple-homeview-a-new-way-to-enjoy-entertainment-and-manage-the-home/), alongside a new Apple TV 4K. Both reach stores on November 13, according to Apple and [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/06/two-more-apple-products-next-week/), which notes it's the last major Apple hardware expected this year.

The wall version needs a power mount made by Schneider Home, which Apple lists from $59.95. HomeView goes on sale in the US, UK, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, India, Singapore and South Africa. Apple says it isn't coming to the EU for now because Siri AI isn't available in iOS there.

## What Apple HomeView does

Think of it as a kitchen-counter screen that's shared by the household but personal to each person. Per Apple:

- **It knows who's looking.** A camera recognizes up to six people by face. Each gets their own home screen, and personal things like Messages, Calendar and Reminders only show for the person standing in front of it.
- **Siri AI is built in.** It's the new, more conversational Siri we covered in our [Siri AI waitlist post](../siri-ai-waitlist/). On HomeView it's labeled a beta, English only to start.
- **Entertainment and calls.** Apple Music, Podcasts, the Apple TV app, some third-party apps, and FaceTime with a camera that keeps you in frame.
- **Smart home controls.** Lights, locks, thermostats, blinds and camera feeds, by touch or voice. It also listens for smoke and carbon monoxide alarms and alerts your iPhone or Apple Watch.

Apple says camera and microphone data are processed in a walled-off part of the A18 chip that, in Apple's words, even Apple can't access. Those are Apple's claims. Independent reviews weren't out when we published.

## What it means for you

HomeView isn't a standalone gadget. Apple's [product page](https://www.apple.com/apple-homeview/) lists what it leans on:

- **An iPhone or iPad.** Setup and multiple users need a compatible iPhone or iPad on the latest software. Your personal content only shows when your paired phone or tablet is on the same Wi-Fi and was recently unlocked.
- **Smart home gear, sold separately.** To control lights or locks, they need to work with Apple Home or Matter, a common smart home standard that lets devices from different brands talk to each other.
- **A plug.** It needs wired power. There's no battery.
- **Usage limits.** Apple says some Siri AI features that run on its servers have daily limits, and that expanded access will cost a fee in the future. It hasn't said what that fee is.

If your house already runs on iPhones, HomeView can work as a shared screen for the family calendar, music and the front-door camera. If you're on Android, it isn't built for you: the personal features depend on Apple devices.

## Is it worth caring about?

Our take, which is opinion: yes, if you're in Apple's world, mostly as a sign of where Apple is heading. Alongside HomeView, Apple is launching what it calls its first lineup of smart home cameras, thermostats and a lock, the Apple Home Collection, starting in December. Together, that's Apple pushing further into the home itself, not just your pocket.

Whether HomeView is good is a question for reviewers once it ships. What's clear today is that it's built around Siri AI, so how useful it feels will depend a lot on how well the new Siri works. (This is context, not a buying recommendation.)
