Meta Muse, Meta's AI agent, is now rolling out inside Instagram DMs. Since November 24, 2026, people in the US, Canada, Mexico, Japan and Singapore can chat with it in a message thread, ask about posts they see, and catch up on missed DMs. In the US and Canada, Instagram also offers to have Muse sort your saved posts.

## What happened with Meta Muse on Instagram

Muse is Meta's AI agent. An agent is an AI that can do tasks for you, not just answer questions, and Muse already had its own standalone app. Now it also shows up in Instagram, as a conversation in your DMs, according to [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/meta-brings-its-muse-ai-assistant-directly-into-instagram-dms/), [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2326663/meta-is-bringing-muse-to-instagram/) and [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/24/meta-muse-instagram-dms/).

Here's what the reports say it can do inside Instagram:

| Feature | What it does | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Muse in DMs | Ask about a post, place or product you see, catch up on DM threads you missed, or share a post and ask Muse to act on it | US, Canada, Mexico, Japan, Singapore (iOS and Android) |
| Saved-post prompt | When you save a post, Instagram suggests letting Muse organize it into Collections | US and Canada |
| Shared history | Your Instagram chats with Muse also appear as a side chat in the Muse app | Same as above |

TechCrunch lists examples like turning a recipe video into a grocery list, turning travel posts into a trip itinerary, and getting outfit ideas from saved fashion reels.

## What it means for you

- **You have to link your accounts.** Engadget reports the features only work for people who have connected their Instagram and Muse accounts. If you've never used Muse, you won't see it doing anything on its own.
- **Meta says nothing happens without your OK.** Per TechCrunch, Meta says "Nothing is shared, published, or sent without their approval," and that you can ask Muse to forget information or undo actions at any time.
- **It's a lighter version.** Engadget says Muse inside Instagram is more limited than the Muse app or website, and you'd likely still need those to see everything Muse does with your Instagram messages.
- **Meta AI isn't going away.** Instagram already has a separate Meta AI chatbot in DMs. An Instagram spokesperson told Engadget it's staying. Meta AI is meant for general questions, while Muse is meant to act more like an assistant. So you may see both.
- **Rollout takes time.** Meta said it begins rolling out on November 24, so it may not be on your phone yet even if you're in one of those countries. WhatsApp's new [scheduled messages](../whatsapp-scheduled-messages/) are arriving the same gradual way.

## Is it worth caring about?

*Our take:* yes, if you use Instagram a lot, because it's a sign of where your everyday apps are heading.

- **It matters most to heavy savers.** If you've bookmarked years of recipes, travel spots and outfits, an assistant that sorts them could be handy. Engadget's reviewer found that useful when testing Muse in its own app.
- **It matters to privacy-minded people too.** An agent that reads your posts and messages is a bigger step than a chatbot you type questions into. Meta's approval rule and the "forget" option are worth knowing about before you link accounts.
- **The bigger picture:** AI agents are moving into the apps people already use. OpenAI built [ChatGPT Wallet](../chatgpt-wallet/) so agents can shop for you, Gemini can [work inside apps on a Mac](../gemini-mac-computer-use/), and [Google AI Mode now connects to Wallet](../google-ai-mode-wallet-drive/). If you're curious why these tools can understand a messy request at all, our explainer on [how ChatGPT works](../how-does-chatgpt-work/) covers the basics.

Like any AI tool, Muse can get things wrong, so it's worth checking a grocery list or itinerary before you rely on it.
